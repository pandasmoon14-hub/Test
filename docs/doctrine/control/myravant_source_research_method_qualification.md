## 1. Purpose

PR2-SRC-B qualifies how Myravant may investigate heterogeneous external
evidence under the source-research architecture established by PR2-SRC-A.

SRC-A establishes the lawful research pipeline and authority boundaries.
SRC-B supplies the research-method layer needed to use that pipeline without
flattening unlike sources, over-analyzing every source, mistaking source
expression for Myravant design, or treating weak evidence as equivalent to
strong direct evidence.

SRC-B is a method contract. It does not authorize bulk source processing,
source acquisition, native content production, conversion, canon promotion,
runtime implementation, agent deployment, or live-play behavior.

The method must remain viable for a corpus of at least 1,000 heterogeneous
sources.

## 2. Ownership boundary

PR2-SRC-B owns only:

- modality-sensitive research-method qualification;
- evidence-mode qualification without a universal numeric confidence score;
- research-depth selection;
- scout versus focused versus deep-analysis rules;
- mechanical-ecology and utilization analysis;
- content-heavy and generator-heavy research method;
- rules-in-use and failure research method;
- software/repository evidence interpretation;
- ordinary-life and institutional evidence method;
- source-local versus cross-source synthesis discipline;
- bounded research work-packet requirements;
- stop, defer, and escalation conditions for research execution.

SRC-B may define what a lawful future research packet must contain.
It may not authorize the packet to run.

## 3. Explicit nonownership

SRC-B does not own:

- originality, rights, similarity, or eligibility adjudication (`PR2-ORG`);
- the final source-analysis-to-design information barrier (`PR2-IR`);
- corpus registry, quotas, genealogy, cultural-bias accounting, novelty
  accounting, saturation, or large-scale batching (`PR2-CORPUS`);
- fiction/LitRPG-specific interpretation (`PR2-FICT`);
- simulation/infrastructure exemplar-specific interpretation (`PR2-SIMEX`);
- legacy source/conversion surface disposition (`PR2-SRC-C`);
- PR2-SRC completion review (`PR2-SRC-D`);
- gameplay doctrine;
- Myravant-native design;
- runtime or production schemas;
- autonomous research-agent orchestration;
- source corpus execution;
- model training;
- live-play or GM behavior.

When a method decision would require one of these authorities, the lawful result
is handoff or escalation.

## 4. Research-unit model

A source is an accountability and provenance unit.

A research unit is the bounded part of evidence actually examined for a
research question.

A research unit may be a chapter or section, mechanic or interaction, table or
generator, bestiary or catalog family, adventure procedure, software component,
repository change, issue or incident, postmortem, observed play pattern,
institution or ordinary-life process, empirical result, narrative phenomenon,
or another bounded evidence slice.

Source identity and research-unit identity must not be collapsed.

One source may yield many research units. One research unit may yield several
observations or pressures. One normalized pressure may be supported,
contradicted, or qualified by many sources.

This many-to-many structure is required before corpus scale.

## 5. Modality qualification

Source modalities are method-selection signals, not authority classes.

They are multi-label rather than mutually exclusive.

A single source may legitimately require several methods.

At minimum, SRC-B recognizes:

1. **rules-heavy**;
2. **content-heavy/catalog**;
3. **generator-heavy/procedural**;
4. **toolkit/point-buy/framework**;
5. **setting/world-heavy**;
6. **scenario/adventure/campaign**;
7. **solo/GM-less/oracle**;
8. **implementation/software**;
9. **failure/postmortem**;
10. **actual-use/community**;
11. **empirical/research**;
12. **ordinary-life/institutional**;
13. **narrative/fiction pressure** — basic identification only; special
    interpretation remains reserved to PR2-FICT;
14. **simulation/infrastructure pressure** — basic identification only; special
    interpretation remains reserved to PR2-SIMEX.

Modality labels do not imply equal evidentiary strength.
Modality labels do not determine Myravant content categories.

## 6. Evidence-mode qualification

Research must distinguish the kind of evidence supporting a claim.

SRC-B recognizes at least:

- **observed implementation**;
- **documented design intent**;
- **observed use**;
- **empirical finding**;
- **failure or incident evidence**;
- **community report or hypothesis**;
- **inferred rationale**;
- **historical evolution evidence**.

These modes may coexist. They must not be silently merged into a single
undifferentiated “source says” claim.

SRC-B does not assign universal numeric confidence scores.

## 7. Minimum claim discipline

Every material source-aware research claim must be capable of carrying:

- source identity;
- bounded locator or evidence location;
- research unit;
- evidence mode;
- claim statement;
- claim scope;
- whether the claim is direct observation, report, or inference;
- material uncertainty;
- known contradiction or counterevidence;
- provenance sufficient to return to the evidence.

A community assertion may be useful as a hypothesis or discovery lead.
It must not silently become empirical fact.

Documentation may describe intent without proving implementation.
Code may prove an implementation path without proving user experience,
maintainability, desirability, or universal best practice.
A postmortem may prove a failure mode without proving that every similar system
will fail.

## 8. Research-depth qualification

Corpus scale requires selective depth.

### 8.1 Scout

Scout identifies modalities, evidence modes, candidate high-information units,
obvious outliers, contradictions, failures, novel pressures, uncertainty, and
routing needs. A scout must not pretend to be a complete source analysis.

### 8.2 Focused analysis

Focused analysis answers one bounded research question, examines only the
evidence necessary for that question, produces attributable observations and
pressures, and stops when the question is answered or blocked.

### 8.3 Deep analysis

Deep analysis is justified by structurally novel pressure, unresolved
contradiction, high interaction density, unusual utilization breadth, likely
cross-family significance, severe failure evidence, important ordinary-life or
institutional pressure, high-consequence uncertainty, or generative structure
that cannot be understood from instances alone.

Deep analysis must examine relationships and lifecycle rather than merely
collect more entries.

### 8.4 Cross-source synthesis

Cross-source synthesis is a separate comparison activity operating on
attributable observations and normalized pressures from multiple evidence
units. It may identify convergence, conflict, outliers, missing capability,
interaction requirements, evidence gaps, or Myravant-facing requirement
candidates.

Synthesis does not erase source provenance.

## 9. Depth-selection law

No source family is automatically entitled to deep analysis.
No source family is automatically restricted to scouting.

Depth should be justified by information value, uncertainty, contradiction,
interaction density, failure significance, novelty, or downstream leverage.

“Important brand,” “large book,” “famous system,” and “many pages” are not
sufficient depth justifications by themselves.

Research should maximize validated information yield rather than pages read.

SRC-B does not define corpus quotas or saturation thresholds.

## 10. Scout contract

A lawful scout result should state what was inspected, source modalities,
evidence modes, candidate pressures, candidate mechanical-ecology
relationships, candidate generative structure, known failure or rules-in-use
evidence, whether deeper analysis is justified, why it is justified, routing
requirements, and what remains unknown.

A scout may return “no material new pressure found.” That is a valid result.

A scout must not create Myravant content to make the source appear productive.

## 11. Mechanical ecology method

Where relevant, analysis should examine relationships among:

- actors;
- targets;
- activities;
- prerequisites;
- permissions;
- prohibitions;
- resources;
- costs;
- risks;
- consequences;
- state changes;
- other mechanics;
- tools and equipment;
- institutions;
- environment;
- social context;
- information availability;
- timing;
- lifecycle;
- scale;
- unusual uses;
- emergent combinations;
- failure conditions.

The goal is not a universal graph schema. The goal is to prevent isolated
headline mechanics from losing the relationships that make them meaningful.

## 12. Utilization method

Research should distinguish capability existence from capability use.

For a material construct, analysis may ask who can use it, who actually uses
it, what else it can be used for, what conditions change its use, whether use
changes across progression or lifecycle, whether ordinary and exceptional
actors use it differently, whether institutions use it differently from
individuals, whether environment or scale changes its meaning, whether users
repurpose it beyond intended use, whether it combines with unrelated systems,
and whether actual use differs from published intent.

Breadth is therefore not raw option count.

## 13. Content-heavy and catalog method

Content instances are evidence about a possibility space.

Research should move upward through:

```text
instance
-> family
-> differentiating dimensions
-> dependency/coherence relationships
-> reusable generative understanding
```

A bestiary may pressure body plan, ecology, behavior, senses, reproduction,
intelligence, social structure, defenses, habitat, scale, metaphysical
exceptions, and interaction patterns.

An equipment catalog may pressure properties, use contexts, constraints,
maintenance, acquisition, failure, compatibility, and identity dimensions.

An NPC catalog may pressure goals, obligations, competencies, relationships,
beliefs, knowledge, habits, affiliations, and current pressures.

A location catalog may pressure function, inhabitants, resources,
infrastructure, history, ecology, access, ownership, hazards, secrets, and
change.

The method must not convert a large catalog into a large Myravant catalog by
default.

## 14. Generator and procedural method

A generator should be studied for the possibility structure it encodes.

Analysis may examine generated dimensions, dependencies, coherence constraints,
weighting or distribution assumptions, exception patterns, invalid
combinations, context sensitivity, state dependence, recursion or composition,
the human function replaced or assisted, what the generator cannot represent,
and pressures visible only in the grammar rather than individual rows.

A table row is not automatically a content artifact.
A distribution in a source is not automatically a Myravant-world prevalence
claim.

## 15. Solo, oracle, and role-substitution method

When a source contains an oracle, automated procedure, AI system, or other
role-substitution mechanism, research should ask what human or institutional
function is being replaced or assisted, what information it receives, what
uncertainty it resolves, what authority it exercises, how it handles ambiguity,
how it fails, what state it must remember, what consequences it can observe,
and what happens when its output conflicts with another authority.

SRC-B records the pressure. It does not adopt the mechanism as Myravant
architecture.

## 16. Rules-in-use method

Where evidence exists, research should preserve the distinction:

```text
published intent
-> implemented rule
-> actual use
-> emergent strategy
-> exploit or failure
-> correction / patch / errata / social response
-> long-term behavior
```

Not every source will provide every stage. Missing stages must remain missing
rather than being invented.

## 17. Failure method

Failure evidence is first-class research evidence.

A failure investigation may ask what failed, at what scale, under what
preconditions, whether the failure was semantic, social, economic, operational,
security, usability, moderation, performance, or maintenance related, whether
it was predicted, how users or operators responded, whether the design was
patched, abandoned, constrained, or accepted, whether it recurred, and what
hidden assumption it exposed.

Failure evidence should produce pressure or falsification conditions, not
automatic prescriptions.

## 18. Software and repository evidence method

At minimum:

- implementation/code shows what one implementation actually does;
- README and architecture documents show intended or claimed design;
- issues and incident reports show failures, user friction, and maintenance
  pressure;
- commit and version history shows architecture evolution and reversal;
- benchmarks show measured behavior under their stated conditions;
- discussions and community reports provide hypotheses, experience reports,
  and discovery leads.

Repository popularity is not architecture authority.

Open-source availability is not evidence that an implementation should be
copied.

An abandoned repository may still provide high-information failure or
maintenance evidence.

## 19. Ordinary-life and institutional method

Ordinary-life research must not be forced through an adventure-mechanic lens.

Relevant analysis may examine roles and responsibilities, recurring activities,
maintenance, resource flows, incentives, obligations, dependency chains,
coordination, conflict, information flow, time horizons, succession, failure
and recovery, access, exclusion, law or policy, social norms, institutional
scale, and effects on nonexceptional actors.

The research output is pressure on world capability and representation.

It is not an instruction to create a mechanic for every observed institution.

## 20. Cross-source synthesis method

Cross-source synthesis should:

1. retain source-local observations and provenance;
2. normalize structurally or functionally comparable pressures;
3. identify where apparently similar pressures are actually unlike;
4. compare constraints, interactions, lifecycle, and utilization;
5. identify convergences;
6. identify contradictions;
7. identify outliers;
8. identify missing evidence;
9. identify interaction requirements;
10. form Myravant-facing requirement candidates only when evidence supports
    abstraction beyond one source.

Repeated source assumptions may increase research confidence or priority.
They do not become doctrine by majority vote.

SRC-B does not own corpus clustering or saturation metrics.

## 21. Contradiction handling

When sources disagree, research must not average away the disagreement.

A contradiction may indicate different problem definitions, actor assumptions,
scales, time horizons, genre constraints, institutional contexts,
implementation limits, evidence quality, or a genuine unresolved tradeoff.

The research result may remain a conflict finding.

## 22. Outlier handling

An outlier is not noise merely because it is rare.

Rare evidence may reveal a missing capability class, hidden assumption, unusual
actor or embodiment, different scale regime, new failure mode, representation
gap, or new interaction class.

SRC-B may route an outlier to deeper analysis. It may not promote rarity into
doctrine simply because it is novel.

## 23. Bounded research packet contract

Any later human- or agent-executed research packet should identify at least:

- `packet_id`;
- parent workstream or research program;
- research question or objective;
- source identity and provenance locator;
- bounded read scope;
- source modality labels;
- requested research mode (`scout`, `focused`, `deep`, or `synthesis`);
- evidence modes expected or encountered;
- lawful output classes;
- prohibited output classes;
- required provenance behavior;
- stop conditions;
- escalation conditions;
- acceptance checks;
- durable checkpoint/output location.

The packet must be reconstructible without relying on one agent's hidden or
ephemeral context.

This is a packet contract, not packet-execution authorization.

## 24. Lawful packet outputs

A packet may produce attributable observations, normalized pressures,
evidence-mode qualifications, mechanical-ecology findings, utilization
findings, generative-dimension findings, failure findings, rules-in-use
findings, outlier findings, contradiction findings, evidence gaps, synthesis
candidates, Myravant-facing requirement candidates, escalation records, or a
valid “no material new pressure” result.

A packet may not produce authoritative Myravant content merely because it was
assigned to a capable model.

## 25. Stop conditions

Research must stop or defer when required evidence is unavailable, the source
does not support the claim, the task would require guessing missing source
content, the task would require inventing Myravant design, source expression
cannot be safely abstracted under current information-barrier controls, the
research question has already been answered to required depth, deeper work is
unlikely to add material validated information, the problem belongs to a
separately controlled successor workstream, or provenance cannot be preserved
sufficiently for audit.

“Stop” is a lawful research result.

## 26. Escalation routes

| Pressure | Route |
| --- | --- |
| originality, rights, similarity, distribution eligibility | `PR2-ORG` |
| source-analysis/design information barrier | `PR2-IR` |
| registry, batching, coverage, genealogy, novelty, saturation, bias | `PR2-CORPUS` |
| fiction/LitRPG-specific interpretation | `PR2-FICT` |
| simulation/infrastructure-specific interpretation | `PR2-SIMEX` |
| legacy extraction/conversion repository disposition | `PR2-SRC-C` |
| PR2-SRC completion determination | `PR2-SRC-D` |
| missing gameplay or framework doctrine | lawful doctrine owner / escalation |
| runtime implementation | applicable runtime gate/workstream |
| canon promotion | canon authority |

SRC-B must not solve an escalation by quietly broadening its own scope.

## 27. Deterministic and semantic tool split

Use deterministic tools where semantic reasoning is unnecessary, including
hashing, path and metadata checks, exact duplicate detection, schema validation,
indexing, counts, exact diffing, and provenance-link validation.

Use high-capability semantic analysis for functional interpretation,
relationship recovery, evidence-mode judgment, contradiction analysis, outlier
analysis, generative-structure interpretation, and cross-source synthesis.

This division is a method principle. It does not authorize agent deployment or
corpus processing.

## 28. Assurance posture

A research conclusion should be no stronger than its evidence.

High-impact requirement candidates should preserve enough evidence separation
for later independent verification.

Where practical, high-impact claims should be pressure-tested by another
evidence source, method, or independent review before becoming relied-upon
requirements.

SRC-B does not create a universal assurance framework or numeric confidence
threshold.

Community consensus alone is not independent verification.

## 29. Anti-patterns

SRC-B rejects reading every source at maximum depth; treating page count,
fame, or repository stars as authority; treating documentation as proof of
implementation; treating community claims as verified fact; treating code as
proof of desirability; counting catalog rows as structural diversity; copying
generator tables rather than studying grammar; ignoring failed or abandoned
approaches; excluding ordinary-life evidence; collapsing intent and
rules-in-use; majority-vote synthesis; source-by-source Myravant production;
storing research truth only in model context; using agents as semantic owners;
and expanding SRC-B to avoid escalation.

## 30. SRC-B acceptance conditions

SRC-B is complete only when:

1. source modalities can select different methods without becoming authority
   classes;
2. materially different evidence modes remain distinguishable;
3. scout, focused, deep, and synthesis modes have distinct purposes;
4. depth is justified by information value rather than source prestige or size;
5. mechanical ecology and utilization can be preserved;
6. content-heavy and generator-heavy evidence can yield generative structure
   rather than copied inventory;
7. rules-in-use and failure evidence have explicit methods;
8. software/repository evidence distinguishes implementation, intent, failure,
   evolution, and community report;
9. ordinary-life/institutional evidence has a lawful method;
10. contradiction and outlier handling preserve rather than flatten pressure;
11. bounded research packets have explicit provenance, scope, outputs, stop,
    escalation, and acceptance requirements;
12. deterministic and semantic work are separated where useful;
13. no bulk source processing or agent execution is authorized;
14. SRC-C, SRC-D, ORG, IR, CORPUS, FICT, SIMEX, R3, runtime, native-content,
    canon, training, and live-play authorities remain separate.
